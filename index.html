Foundational research

Seller Lifecycle

A shared framework for seller strategy.

SegmentationResearch adoptionStrategic planning

The question

Etsy lacked a shared model for how seller goals evolved over time and with operational scale. Teams alternated between one-size-fits-all assumptions and a hobbyist-versus-professional binary.

My approach

I identified gaps in prior segmentation and framed a mixed-method initiative around sellers’ growth motivations. I partnered with Analytics on behavioral and sales data, directed external qualitative research, and translated the findings into a framework for Product and executive leadership. Later refreshes brought new evidence into the model.

The decision it enabled

We mapped product initiatives and investments to lifecycle stages, enabling more targeted prioritization and evaluation. As the segmentation evolved, I worked with Analytics leadership to preserve the strategic narrative while integrating new evidence.

The central insight

Success creates motivation to invest.

Growth in shop sales became the primary organizing principle for seller product strategy.

Platform success→Seller motivation→Effort & investment

Frame the question

A hobbyist-versus-professional split described sellers too broadly to guide investment. I reframed the work around motivation, growth, and the support sellers needed as their businesses developed.

Connect the evidence

The work combined attitudinal survey evidence, behavioral and sales data, and qualitative research. My role included setting the research direction, interpreting the findings with partners, and building the strategic narrative.

Make it usable

The framework gave teams a way to connect initiatives to seller needs at different stages. Refreshing it with Analytics helped preserve a shared language while allowing the evidence to evolve.

Gift Mode

Reframing shopping around the gift recipient.

Generative researchConcept developmentExecutive alignment

The question

Many Etsy buyers came for gifting, but recommendations, prompts, and discovery reflected the shopper’s preferences rather than the recipient’s needs.

My approach

I originated and led an innovation pod of two designers, two researchers, and an analyst, with direct visibility to the CPO. I conducted generative research and helped develop the recipient-first framework. I led the team through concept development, early testing, opportunity sizing, and roadmap planning, then co-developed a Product and Marketing brief for the CEO.

The decision it enabled

The behavioral framework aligned Product and Marketing around one customer premise, secured executive approval for a cross-functional initiative, and established a repeatable innovation model.

The central insight

Put the recipient at the center.

Gifting is a distinct behavioral mode requiring tailored discovery, personalization, and messaging.

Offsite Ads

Resolving a sensitive participation decision.

Mixed methodsChoice modelingPrice sensitivity

The question

Etsy needed broad seller participation in Offsite Ads to offer a compelling inventory feed for Google Product Listing Ads. The existing opt-in model constrained coverage and revenue potential.

My approach

I led the research program with the CFO as executive sponsor. The team combined qualitative interviews with choice modeling; I guided the framing, interpretation, and recommendation. We considered risks separately: opting out of ads, reducing investment, and leaving the marketplace were different possible responses.

The decision it enabled

The research informed a tiered model: required participation for high-volume sellers and opt-out flexibility for smaller sellers. I helped leadership evaluate the tradeoff between marketplace coverage and sellers’ differing sensitivities to price and control.

The central insight

One rule would not fit every seller.

Distinct price-sensitivity thresholds made seller agency and marketplace trust central to program design.

Customer experience

Making feedback useful in day-to-day product work.

Experience measurementSurvey researchProduct partnership

The question

How could ongoing buyer and seller feedback help teams evaluate specific product experiences, alongside the behavioral KPIs they already used?

My approach

I led work on buyer and seller experience measurement, including trackers, driver analysis, and guidance on how teams should interpret the measures. I partnered directly with product squads to test their use in product evaluation.

Putting it to work

In July 2020, we completed a pilot with the Listing Videos prototype group. A second iteration for order cancellations was underway later that year. These pilots tested how customer experience measures could complement behavioral indicators in specific product contexts.

What changed

Teams gained practical guidance on when to use customer experience metrics, when not to use them, and how to interpret them in squad work. I continued refining tracker measures and drivers as teams applied the approach.

The working principle

A metric needs a decision it can inform.

Tracking sentiment is a starting point. The next step is helping a team understand what a measure can tell it about a particular experience—and where other evidence is needed.

Research leadership

Give people room to grow.

I want researchers to build judgment, take on bigger questions, and become people their partners turn to for help.

I notice people who might not be on everyone’s radar yet, and give them real chances.

I put people into rooms they haven’t been in before and stay close enough to help when they need it. Over time, they start doing that work without me—which is the point.

At Etsy, my remit grew from quantitative research to Product & UX Research and, later, responsibility for the full research organization.

Develop managers and senior ICs

I coached two individual contributors into management and mentored a researcher toward Senior Staff. Development meant stretch opportunities, concrete feedback, and support tailored to the path each person wanted to take.

Stay close to the work

I helped researchers frame studies, interpret evidence, and navigate partner relationships while giving them ownership of the work. My role was to help them make stronger decisions and build the confidence to make those decisions independently.

Build the conditions for good research

I managed research tools, vendors, and budgets, owned the Qualtrics platform, and developed reusable methods and coaching for researchers, product managers, and designers.

Strategic foresight · Executive influence

AI and the creative marketplace.

Recognizing an emerging shift and helping leadership consider what it meant for Etsy.

Spotting the shift

I identified AI’s growing importance to the creative process and brought its implications to Etsy’s Executive Team. The initial work was trendspotting: recognizing a change that would matter to a marketplace built around creativity.

Influencing the decision

The COO later told me this input helped leadership agree that AI-based items should be allowed. I subsequently advocated for clearer disclosures and policy guidance around AI’s role in the creative process.

Policy context

Etsy’s 2024 seller policy required disclosure when an item was created using AI. Further policy updates were announced in October 2026, after I had left.

My contribution was early issue identification, executive input, and advocacy during my tenure.
